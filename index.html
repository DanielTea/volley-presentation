Why “Volley”

Many neurons, firing in turn, follow a signal none could follow alone.

In the auditory nerve no single neuron can fire fast enough to follow a high tone. Groups of them fire in staggered volleys, and together they keep up with the signal (Wever & Bray, 1930).

Volley does the same for a factory: many small spiking units, each firing sparsely, that together follow every stream of every asset, without ever stopping.

01 Architecture

A spike-only stack that reads a stream.

Volley's core is the spiking stack that Control-F has measured release by release since v11 under the name ShuntFormer. It has no attention blocks: eleven levels, each a state read and a layer of threshold cells, pass whole spikes through a 512-channel stream. v17 wraps it with telemetry encoders and a memory capsule per asset.

02 Streaming

Windows forget. Streams remember.

A Transformer reads in windows. Everything older than its window is gone, and its weights stop changing once it ships. Volley reads one unbroken stream per asset, carries its state forward, forgets gradually and keeps learning. Telemetry never ends, so a model that reads a stream fits the problem.

Unbroken streams

One step per sample, state carried forward. A stream cut anywhere fires exactly the spikes of one pass over the whole of it, so any asset can be paused, checkpointed and resumed without re-reading its history.

Measured · v15–v16

Reads beyond its training

Trained on windows of 2,048 bytes, the stack reads 8,192 cells back, four times further, with no new synapses, and scores 0.013 bits per byte better for it.

Measured · v16

Forgets the way memory does

Beyond the window a read's charge falls by a learned slope for every doubling of age: forgetting by a power law, as in human memory. 79 of 88 heads learned a slope that keeps the far past bounded at any reach.

Measured · v16

Learns while it infers

Fast synapses start at zero in every stream, and each arrival teaches them by a local rule in whole numbers, with no gradient and no retraining. On books their gain grows as the square root of what was read.

Measured · v15–v16

Event-driven by design

Whole spikes and synapses on an 8-bit grid cost 0.0008 bits per byte against floating point. A steady signal sends few spikes. The design targets edge and neuromorphic silicon. The native integer decoder and its energy audit come next.

Design target

One model, many memories

All assets share the slow weights, and each stream gets a small capsule of its own. That personalises the model per asset without fine-tuning, retraining or a vector database.

03 Industrial AI

One nervous system per asset.

Volley v17 brings the stream to the shop floor. Every pump, spindle, compressor and conveyor gets its own memory capsule. The capsule learns that asset's normal: the load cycle of its shifts, its warm-up after a start, the slow drift of its seasons. The same model answers questions about the asset in plain language.

  1. 1

    Sense

    Telemetry arrives as spike events, stream by stream, at its own rate.

  2. 2

    Predict

    Before each sample arrives, Volley predicts it from this asset's own history.

  3. 3

    Surprise

    The bits a sample costs are its surprise, measured against this asset's normal rather than a fleet threshold.

  4. 4

    Teach

    The arrival teaches the fast synapses in whole numbers. Inference is training.

  5. 5

    Answer

    Operators ask in language, and the answer is read from the same live state.

Surprise is the anomaly score. The error spikes that teach Volley are the same ones that tell you something changed, often long before a static alarm limit trips.

    05 Measured today

    Benchmarked in the open, one release at a time.

    Volley's language core is measured against a full-attention Transformer of the same size and the same training on held-out text, every release, with paired intervals and the numbers that go against it.

    1.120 bits per byte on the full enwik8 test split, read as one unbroken stream (v16)
    0.013 bits per byte behind a Transformer trained on the same 2,048-byte windows. We publish that gap.
    0.0008 bits per byte: the whole cost of whole spikes and 8-bit synapses against floating point
    368.64M training bytes from random weights, the same for both models

    What is and is not shown yet

    • Measured: language modelling on enwik8 and PG-19: streams, reach, forgetting slopes and fast synapses (ShuntFormer v11–v16).
    • v17 design target: telemetry delta encoders, per-asset capsules and language Q&A over live streams. Its benchmarks will be published like every release before it.
    • Not yet: a native integer decoder and an energy audit on hardware. Today the spike races are computed in closed form on GPUs and CPUs.

    06 Roadmap

    From a byte model to a factory's nervous system.

    1. v11–v14

      Spiking byte decoders

      Fast synapses written by error spikes, read heads on a tape of the stream, state reads, race cells and read clocks.

    2. v15

      A stack that reads a stream

      Eleven levels of state reads and threshold cells. As a stream, level with a same-size Transformer on enwik8 test: 1.141 against 1.144.

    3. v16

      Long streams

      Learned power-law forgetting, 4× reach beyond training, and a Transformer trained on equal windows to measure against.

    4. v17 · Volley

      Language + telemetry, per asset · SPS 2026 preview

      Delta spike encoders for sensor streams, a memory capsule per asset and stream, fast synapses trained in the loop at full size, and questions in plain language over live streams.

    5. Next

      On the edge

      Native integer decoder and pulse audit, neuromorphic and edge targets, and pilot deployments on partner lines.
